# P5.3 — `roomd`

> Live presence for many people in one room, on Durable Objects, with an honest answer about what happens when the connection drops.

**Phase 5** · ships week 30 · Cloudflare Workers + Durable Objects · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P5.3`

## Why this one

[`P1.4`](../../phase-1/projects/P1.4.md) answered which transport to pick and left the
scaling question explicitly for this phase. `roomd` picks it up: one Durable Object per room
holding authoritative state, WebSocket clients with heartbeats, reconnect-with-resume, and a
written answer to what happens when hundreds of people join one room. That last part is the
project: anyone can broadcast to two tabs, but the interesting work is what happens when a
client is slow, when a socket dies mid-session, and when one room is too popular for one
actor. It lands here for a second reason too. Weeks 27–29 were deliberately unglamorous;
this is the phase's most fun build, and two browsers moving in sync is a demo you will want
to show someone — which at month 7 is worth more than another measurement exercise.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have written a single-writer coordination point yourself and can explain why one
  authoritative actor per room removes a class of race conditions.
- You will have built reconnect-with-resume and can explain what the client must remember
  for a reconnect to be a resume rather than a reset.
- You will be able to argue sticky sessions against a pub/sub backplane — the classic
  realtime scaling question — from something you built rather than a diagram you read,
  including what you chose to do about a slow client.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **Reconnect works locally because nothing is ever really lost** — a refresh is not a
  network drop, so your resume path passes every test you thought to write and fails on a
  train. → cut the connection at the transport level rather than reloading the page, and
  make the client prove it resumed from a sequence number instead of re-fetching everything.
- **Presence is empty after an idle stretch** — a Durable Object can be evicted between
  messages, so in-memory state is the wrong home for the participant list. → treat memory as
  a cache of the object's own storage and rebuild on wake. Whether hibernation is on your
  path is platform behaviour to confirm in [`../verified/`](../verified/), not to assume.
- **Latency climbs as the room fills** — one object is a single-threaded actor and broadcast
  cost is per-connection per-message, so a room that is fine with ten participants degrades
  at a few hundred. That is not a bug to fix inside the hour budget; it is the honest README
  section the spec asks for. → measure where it turns, write it down, describe the fan-out
  you would build.
- **Cursor updates at frame rate** — chasing smoothness can eat the whole week. → coalesce
  and rate-limit on the sender. The 2-hour rule fires the moment you are tuning
  interpolation instead of finishing the reconnect path.

## The interview angle

Stateful realtime is a topic most candidates have only read about. This gives you why a
single authoritative actor per room is easier to reason about than shared state behind a
load balancer, what sticky sessions and a backplane each cost, and why presence is harder
than messaging. Resume line: *live multi-user presence on one authoritative actor per room,
with heartbeats, reconnect-with-resume and documented backpressure.* The follow-up is "what
about a room bigger than one actor?" — and the honest answer is that you did not shard it:
describe the fan-out design, and say conflict resolution was cut on purpose.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no CRDT or operational transform (a project of its own, and it would eat
the week); no auth (orthogonal, and phase 3 already did it properly); no persistence beyond
the object's own storage; no mobile client.

**Stretch** — add a second region and show the latency difference. Only after the ship
checklist is green; a stretch goal that eats the ship is a failure, not an upgrade.
