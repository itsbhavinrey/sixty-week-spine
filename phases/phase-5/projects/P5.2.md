# P5.2 — `driftwatch` · driftwatch layer 6

> Scanning moves off the request path - with a duplicate delivery you can prove you survive.

**Phase 5** · ships week 29 · Cloudflare Workers + Queues · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P5.2`

## Why this one

Until now a `driftwatch` scan only happens because someone is waiting for it: the work lives
inside one HTTP request, so it can only last as long as a client's patience. This layer
breaks that coupling. `POST /scans` returns 202 Accepted and enqueues; a consumer does the
registry work afterwards. Once scans stop being request-scoped, the service can crawl
repositories on a schedule rather than only when asked — which is what the scheduled Drift
Index in [`C1`](../../phase-8/projects/C1.md) runs on. It comes after
[`P4.3`](../../phase-4/projects/P4.3.md) on purpose: you want traces in place *before* you
make work invisible, because a queue with no observability is where jobs disappear quietly.
It also sets up the outbox in [`P7.1`](../../phase-7/projects/P7.1.md).

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have written an idempotent consumer yourself and can explain why at-least-once
  plus idempotency is the production standard, and why exactly-once delivery is unachievable
  at the network layer.
- You will have retried with exponential backoff and full jitter, and can say what happens
  to a struggling downstream when every consumer retries on the same rhythm.
- You will have poisoned a message, watched it reach a dead-letter queue, and replayed it
  from an endpoint you wrote.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **The queue swallowed it** — a scan never finishes and there is no error anywhere, because
  you moved work to a place where failure has no caller to complain to. → carry
  `traceparent` into the consumer so one trace spans producer and consumer; if the consumer
  starts its own trace you have two halves and no link. This is why
  [`P4.3`](../../phase-4/projects/P4.3.md) came first.
- **Your idempotency key is not idempotent** — a retry writes a second row, because the key
  came from something that changes per delivery (a message identifier, a timestamp) rather
  than from the scan. → key on the scan and enforce it with a uniqueness constraint in the
  database, not an `if` in the handler: check-then-write loses under concurrency and looks
  correct in every single-threaded test.
- **Local emulation and the deployed queue disagree** — redelivery timing and batching are
  the usual divergence, so a test passes locally and the duplicate only shows up deployed. →
  the 2-hour rule fires here: write "I expect X, I observe Y", reduce to the smallest
  failing delivery, then read the platform's own documentation, not a post about queues in
  general. A heavy replay loop also eats metered queue operations — the ceiling is in
  [`../verified/`](../verified/).
- **The wall.** [`plan/03-reality-check.md`](../../../plan/03-reality-check.md) calls weeks
  26–30 the structural attrition risk, and queues are the least glamorous thing in the plan:
  no UI, no screenshot worth showing, six months in, still short of the model layer that
  made you want to do this. Most plans of this length die in month 7, not month 1. The
  failure is motivational and the symptom is weeks that quietly produce nothing. One
  sanctioned escape: pull `streamgate` ([`P6.1`](../../phase-6/projects/P6.1.md)) forward
  into week 33's buffer — it costs almost nothing dependency-wise and buys an LLM win
  exactly when you need one.

## The interview angle

The queue conversation, and it goes deep fast: at-least-once versus exactly-once, where the
idempotency key comes from, what a dead-letter queue is for, why jitter exists, and how you
knew the consumer kept up. Resume line: *moved dependency scanning off the request path onto
a queue with an idempotent consumer, jittered retries and a replayable dead-letter path.*
The follow-up is "how do you know the database write and the enqueue can't diverge?" — and
the honest answer is that you don't yet: the outbox is the stretch goal, and lands properly
in [`P7.1`](../../phase-7/projects/P7.1.md).

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no Kafka (phase 7; doing both here means learning two brokers badly); no
saga or orchestration layer (multi-step compensation is a project, not a feature); no
priority queues; no scheduling beyond one daily scan.

**Stretch** — a transactional outbox so the database write and the enqueue cannot diverge.
Only after the ship checklist is green.
