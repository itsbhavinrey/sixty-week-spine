# P3.3 — `passkey-drill`

> Passkey registration and authentication, ceremony by ceremony, with the fallback flow nobody builds.

**Phase 3** · ships week 19 · Cloudflare Workers + Neon · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P3.3`

## Why this one

Passkeys are what the industry is migrating toward, and the number of engineers who have
implemented both ceremonies by hand is small enough that doing it is a real differentiator.
The demos online stop at "it worked on my laptop"; the part that matters in production is
the fallback — what a user does on a borrowed machine, or after losing the device that held
the credential. It sits at week 19 because [`P3.1`](P3.1.md) gave you the session to
establish once a ceremony succeeds and [`P3.2`](P3.2.md) got you used to reading a spec
instead of a wrapper, and because [`P3.4`](P3.4.md) needs a working passkey login next week
to put on the spine. Of the four projects here it has the most current interview leverage
and the least settled tooling, so expect to read spec text more than usual.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have verified an attestation and an assertion signature yourself, and can
  explain what the challenge stops and why replay is not possible.
- You will have implemented the signature counter check, and can say what it is for and
  when it legitimately does not move.
- You will have built the fallback and recovery path, and can explain why a passkey-only
  system with no recovery story is not shippable.

## Build plan

| # | Milestone | Roughly | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relying-party config, challenge issue and storage | 1 hr | A challenge is minted per attempt, stored, and expires |
| 2 | Registration ceremony + credential storage | 3 hrs | A real passkey on your own device registers and persists |
| 3 | Authentication: assertion, signature, counter | 3 hrs | Login succeeds; a tampered assertion is refused with a reason |
| 4 | Fallback flow + device-management screen | 3 hrs | A user with no passkey still gets in, and can remove a credential |

**Definition of done** — a deployed URL where you register a real passkey and sign in with
it on video, both ceremonies drawn as sequence diagrams, a device-management screen, and
one honest paragraph on account recovery; the ship checklist in
[`../README.md`](../README.md) green.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **The ceremony silently refuses to start** — the browser API is strict about the relying
  party identifier matching the page's origin, about secure transport, and about being
  called from a real user gesture, and it rejects rather than explains. → Settle the origin
  topology before you write any verification logic. Most early failures have this shape, and
  the 2-hour rule fires fast here: reduce to one page, one button, one registration call.
- **Verification that passes for the wrong reason** — it is easy to decode the response,
  read the fields and never actually check the signature, and everything looks fine because
  your own device is honest. → Write a deliberately tampered assertion into a test and make
  sure it fails. If it passes, you built a login screen, not authentication.
- **Platform behaviour you cannot fix** — which authenticators are offered, whether a
  credential syncs, and how the prompt appears are decided by the platform, not by you. →
  Test on two devices, write down what differed, and put that in the README instead of
  trying to normalize it. Check [`../verified/`](../verified/) for the spec level and the
  adoption data before citing either; [`../topics.md`](../topics.md) lists both.

## The interview angle

"How do passkeys work" is asked increasingly and rarely answered past "public key
cryptography"; you will name both ceremonies and what the server verifies in each. The
system-design rep is account recovery — a credential that cannot leave the device forces
you to reason about the weakest alternate path, which is where real breaches happen. Resume
line: *WebAuthn registration and authentication implemented against the spec, with the
fallback and device-management flows most demos skip.* The follow-up is attestation: "how do
you know it's real hardware?" You cut certificate validation against a vendor list, because
it needs infrastructure this project has no budget for and most consumer deployments skip
it too.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no attestation-certificate validation against a vendor list, no
enterprise attestation, no cross-device linking UI. Each pulls in trust infrastructure that
would eat the week, and none is needed to prove the ceremonies.

**Stretch** — conditional UI (autofill) passkey sign-in. Only after the ship checklist is
green; it is polish, and it rests on platform behaviour you do not control.
