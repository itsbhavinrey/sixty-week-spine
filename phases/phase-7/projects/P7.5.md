# P7.5 — `sysdesign-reps`

> Twelve system design write-ups with real numbers, real diagrams, and a 'what I'd cut' section on each.

**Phase 7** · ships week 52 · GitHub Pages · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P7.5`

## Why this one

This is the only project in the plan whose artifact is writing rather than running code, and it is
the payoff for the drill hours that run from week 44 to week 58. Twelve designs — shortener, rate
limiter, feed, chat, notification fan-out, dedupe, idempotency layer, leaderboard, file upload,
search, multi-tenancy, cost model — each with requirements and scale assumptions, a diagram, the
data model, the bottleneck, the trade-off you took, and a "what I'd cut if I had half the time"
section. It ships in week 52 because it accumulates: the reps start in week 44 and weeks 50 and 51
exist to write them, so this is the one project you cannot compress into its ship week. Phase 7 adds
nothing to the spine — `driftwatch` is untouched between
[`P6.7`](../../phase-6/projects/P6.7.md) and [`C1`](../../phase-8/projects/C1.md) — and of the two
things this phase buys, vocabulary and reps, this is the reps. Several designs describe systems you
built: the rate limiter, the idempotency layer and dedupe are
[`P5.2`](../../phase-5/projects/P5.2.md) and [`P7.1`](P7.1.md) written up, which is why those will
be your strongest three.

Applications start in week 57. This is the last artifact finished before strangers read the
portfolio, and it is the one that reads as judgement rather than throughput.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have produced your own scale estimates twelve times and can go from a stated daily active
  count to storage, bandwidth and queries per second out loud, without stalling.
- You will have chosen and defended a trade-off in writing repeatedly, so the interview version is
  recall rather than improvisation.
- You will have named the bottleneck before designing around it, which is the habit that separates a
  design from a diagram.
- You will have written twelve short design documents, which is the RFC skill senior work runs on,
  in a form you can point a hiring manager at.

## Build plan

This one is a cadence, not a sequence. Hold the rhythm and the artifact assembles itself.

| # | Milestone | Roughly | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Template plus the published index, empty | first hour | Six headings fixed, the site builds and deploys with zero designs |
| 2 | Reps running alongside the phase's builds, weeks 44–49 | ~3 hrs/week | Four designs written, each timeboxed to about ninety minutes |
| 3 | Reps 5–8 in week 50's writing block | ~6 hrs | Eight published; the estimation arithmetic has stopped being slow |
| 4 | Reps 9–12 in week 51 | ~6 hrs | Twelve published, each with its assumptions stated up front |
| 5 | Pass over all twelve in week 52 | ~3 hrs | Every "what I'd cut" is specific, and where you disagree with the standard answer is cited |

**Definition of done** — the ship checklist in [`../README.md`](../README.md) is green, the site is
live, and every design states the numbers it assumed and why. A design without numbers does not
count toward twelve.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **The first two take four hours each.** Estimation is slow before it is fast, and a blank
  assumptions section stalls everything downstream → fix one set of default assumptions in the
  template and adjust per design rather than deriving from nothing each time. Timebox hard; an
  over-long design is a worse artifact, not a better one.
- **They start reading like the standard answers.** Reading a canonical solution before writing your
  own replaces your reasoning with someone else's, and an interviewer can hear it → write first,
  compare after, record the disagreement. The disagreement is the part with value.
- **Diagrams eat the time.** Boxes and arrows are infinitely polishable → keep them in text-based
  source in the repo, cap each at one screen, and treat prose as the deliverable.
- **The last four slip into week 52 and collide with the drill push.** Week 52 also carries the
  final DSA stretch, so a backlog of four unwritten designs has nowhere to go → publish eight good
  ones and the remaining four as stubs with assumptions only, rather than leaving the site
  unpublished. Two hours stuck on one design means pick a different one and come back.

## The interview angle

There is no translation step here — this is the round. India product-company loops test system
design at mid-level, and you will walk in having written twelve, several about systems you built and
can show. The one that carries the most weight is whichever design you can open with "I built a
smaller version of this, here is what actually broke." Resume line: *twelve published system design
write-ups with stated scale assumptions, data models, named bottlenecks and an explicit cut list on
each.* The follow-up an interviewer reaches for is the "what I'd cut" section — they pick one and
ask what you would do with the other half of the time, which you have already answered in writing.
Where a design has no implementation behind it, say so; the ones that do will carry the loop.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no implementations, no problem-solving practice inside this repo, no reproducing
a course's answers. Building any one of these would cost more than all twelve write-ups and prove
less about design judgement.

**Stretch** — record a short whiteboard walkthrough of your two best designs and put them on the
repo. Almost nobody does this, and it is the closest thing in the plan to being interviewed before
the interview. After the twelve are published only.
