# P7.1 — `outbox`

> A database write and a Kafka event that cannot diverge, plus a duplicate delivery you survive on purpose.

**Phase 7** · ships week 45 · Docker Compose + make demo + asciinema · difficulty High
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P7.1`

## Why this one

In [`P5.2`](../../phase-5/projects/P5.2.md) `driftwatch` began writing a scan row and enqueueing a
job in one request — two systems, no shared transaction. That holds until the process dies between
the two statements, and then you own either a row nobody will process or a job whose row does not
exist. This project reproduces that divergence deliberately and removes it: the event goes into an
outbox table inside the same transaction as the data, and a separate CDC process moves it to Kafka
afterwards. It sits in week 45 because week 44 is the Kafka week, and because the idempotent
consumer and DLQ you wrote against Cloudflare Queues are the pieces you now rebuild against a log.
Phase 7 buys interview vocabulary and system-design reps, not spine features — `driftwatch` is
untouched between [`P6.7`](../../phase-6/projects/P6.7.md) and the capstone
[`C1`](../../phase-8/projects/C1.md). The outbox is the exception: C1 composes it.

There is no free managed Kafka, so no live URL. `docker compose up` + `make demo` + an asciinema
cast is the proof, and the spec calls that a completely legitimate one. The container work from
[`P4.1`](../../phase-4/projects/P4.1.md) makes standing the topology up cheap.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have written the dual-write bug and its fix yourself, and can explain why "commit, then
  publish" is not a fix and why the outbox row is all the transaction can guarantee.
- You will have moved rows out of a table with CDC and can explain the difference between polling
  the outbox and tailing the database's replication stream.
- You will have forced a duplicate delivery and survived it, and can say in one sentence why
  exactly-once delivery is a lie — this phase's exit criterion.
- You will have run the same topology on a managed queue and on a partitioned log, and can say which
  you would pick for a given ordering requirement.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **The broker is up and your client cannot reach it.** A broker advertises an address to its
  clients, and inside the Compose network and from your host those differ → set the advertised
  listeners per network path, and debug with one producer and one topic, not the demo.
- **The CDC connector starts and streams nothing, silently.** Change capture needs the database
  configured for replication and a slot the connector owns; a missing one reads as no data rather
  than an error → verify the slot exists before touching connector config. Two hours of connector
  config with no messages is where the 2-hour rule should fire.
- **The duplicate test passes for the wrong reason.** If nothing was actually redelivered, an
  idempotent consumer is untested → acknowledge after the side effect and kill the consumer in
  between, then assert the effect count, not the absence of an error.
- **Exactly-once creeps back in.** The log offers guarantees that look like it → they cover the log,
  not your database. Keep the claim out of the README; the restraint is the signal.

## The interview angle

"How do you avoid dual-write inconsistency" is a standard round, and this is the answer with a
recording attached. The system-design rep buried here is the dedupe and idempotency pair, two of the
twelve write-ups in [`P7.5`](P7.5.md). Resume line: *transactional outbox with change-data capture
into Kafka, an idempotent consumer proven against forced duplicate delivery, and a dead letter
queue.* The follow-up is why not distributed transactions, or the log's own transactional semantics
— both were cut: one buys coordination you cannot afford, the other does not span your database.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no stream processing, no schema registry, no exactly-once claims, no multi-broker
tuning. Each pulls the week toward operating a log rather than proving one pattern, and the pattern
is the deliverable.

**Stretch** — a small aggregation over the stream, or the same topology on quorum queues with a
comparison. Only after the checklist is green; this project is flagged High and has no slack.
