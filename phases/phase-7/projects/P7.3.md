# P7.3 — `raftlet`

> Three nodes, one leader, and a network partition that doesn't produce two of them.

**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P7.3`

## Why this one

**Read the cut criterion first.** [`plan/03-reality-check.md`](../../../plan/03-reality-check.md)
says it plainly: "If you're consistently 20% over, don't compress — **cut P7.3 (`raftlet`) and the
stretch goals**. That's ~25 hours of slack that costs you almost nothing in hireability." This is
the one project in the whole plan flagged optional, and it is flagged that way on purpose. If you
arrived at week 47 behind, skip it, take the week back, and close this file now.

If the schedule allows it, the case is honest and narrow. Consensus is the most interesting thing in
phase 7 and the least likely to come up at the mid-level bar — nobody will ask you to derive leader
election. What it buys is that the log underneath the broker you just used in [`P7.1`](P7.1.md), and
the coordination underneath every lock and lease you have relied on, stop being facts you repeat and
become mechanisms you have watched fail. Week 47's focus — quorums, consistency models, the
availability trade — is where that lands. Phase 7 buys vocabulary and reps, not spine features:
`driftwatch` is untouched between [`P6.7`](../../phase-6/projects/P6.7.md) and
[`C1`](../../phase-8/projects/C1.md), and this is the most purely vocabulary-buying of the five.

There is no live URL for the cluster — three coordinating nodes are not a serverless workload.
`docker compose up` + `make demo` plus a Pages-hosted visualizer of the recorded run is the proof,
and the spec calls that completely legitimate. The container work from
[`P4.1`](../../phase-4/projects/P4.1.md) is why it is a small job.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have implemented leader election yourself and can explain what a term is, why a candidate
  needs a majority rather than a plurality, and what randomised timeouts prevent.
- You will have replicated a log across nodes and can explain when an entry becomes committed and
  why the leader cannot trust its own tail.
- You will have partitioned your own cluster and can show, from a test rather than a claim, why the
  minority side cannot elect a second leader.
- You will be able to state what your version does not do — the part of the README that reads senior.

not.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **Everyone is a candidate forever.** Fixed timeouts make nodes campaign in lockstep and split the
  vote every round → randomise the election timeout per node, and log every term change so you can
  see the livelock rather than guess at it.
- **Your partition is not a partition.** Stopping a process is a crash, not a network split, and
  crashes do not produce the interesting bug → drop messages at your transport layer while leaving
  every node running, and make which links are cut an explicit input to the test.
- **The commit rule is subtly wrong and nothing notices.** Replication that acknowledges too early
  passes every happy-path test → derive the rule from the majority count and write the test that
  fails when you relax it. Three hours into a replication edge case, the 2-hour rule has already
  fired: this is the project the plan told you to cut, and shipping the election with an honest "log
  replication is partial" note beats losing the week.
- **Async timing makes tests flaky.** Real timers turn correctness bugs into intermittent ones →
  drive the clock from the test, so a failure is reproducible.

## The interview angle

Nobody will ask you to implement this. What it changes is the quality of your answer when someone
asks why a system needs an odd number of nodes, what happens to writes on the minority side of a
split, or why a leader lease has a term. The system-design rep is the consistency-model choice that
runs through half of [`P7.5`](P7.5.md)'s write-ups. Resume line:
*teaching implementation of leader election and log replication with a partition-injection harness
and a test proving no split-brain.* The follow-up is what production consensus adds — snapshots,
membership changes, durable state — and "I cut all three, this is a learning artifact" is the
correct answer, said without apology.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no snapshotting, no membership changes, no durability guarantees, no performance
work. Say so in the README; a toy that claims to be a database is worse than a toy.

**Stretch** — log compaction, or a deliberately introduced bug that produces split-brain shipped
alongside the test that catches it. Note that this project's stretch goals are named in the same
sentence as the project itself as the first thing to cut.
