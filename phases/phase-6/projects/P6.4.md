# P6.4 — `driftwatch` · driftwatch layer 7

> Ask 'what actually breaks if I go from React Router 7 to 8?' and get a cited answer built from the real release notes.

**Phase 6** · ships week 39 · Neon pgvector + Cloudflare Workers · difficulty High
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P6.4`

## Why this one

Every upgrade decision starts with someone reading release notes badly. This layer reads them
properly and answers the question with citations to the exact changelog line — the problem the
audit in [`plan/00-currency-audit.md`](../../../plan/00-currency-audit.md) solved by hand, so you
already know the task is real and what a good answer looks like. It is the first layer of the
spine that *answers* rather than reports: the CLI, schema, public API, auth, traces and queue all
state facts, and this one takes a question. It sits after [`P6.3`](P6.3.md) on purpose — a
reranker without a golden dataset cannot be shown to have helped — and across two weeks because
it is the biggest single build in the plan. [`P6.5`](P6.5.md) exposes it to agents,
[`P6.6`](P6.6.md) acts on its evidence, [`P6.7`](P6.7.md) cuts its bill, and the capstone
[`C1`](../../phase-8/projects/C1.md) cites it as the retrieval half of the spine.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have built an ingestion and chunking pipeline yourself and can explain what metadata
  enrichment buys at retrieval time, not just at embedding time.
- You will have run hybrid retrieval — keyword search and vector search merged with reciprocal
  rank fusion, then reranked — and can say what each stage contributed, with a score.
- You will have produced answers with inline citations and can explain how you keep a citation
  attached to a claim rather than bolted on afterwards.
- You will be able to describe, from your own measurements, three retrieval failures your system
  has — which is the part of this repo that reads as senior.

## Where you'll get stuck

**This is a flagged overrun.** [`plan/03-reality-check.md`](../../../plan/03-reality-check.md)
is explicit: two projects will take twice their estimate, almost certainly
[`P3.2`](../../phase-3/projects/P3.2.md) (OAuth) and this one. Both are flagged and both are
split across weeks. It also names the property that makes RAG different: it appears to work
almost immediately and is very hard to make actually good. You will have something answering
questions in four hours and then spend twelve more discovering the answers are subtly wrong.
That gap is where people conclude they are not smart enough. It arrives at month 9, when initial
momentum is long gone. None of that is a warning to be brave about; it is a schedule you plan
around, four ways:

- You built [`P6.3`](P6.3.md) first for exactly this reason. "It feels wrong" becomes a score,
  and a number you can move is a completely different experience from a vibe you cannot.
- Ship the naive version at the end of week 38 and deploy it. Broken-and-deployed beats
  perfect-and-local, and it fixes the ordering of the remaining work.
- If week 39 slips, cut the reranker to the stretch goal and keep the evals. The evals are the
  differentiating skill; the reranker is a technique.
- The "where retrieval fails" section turns frustration into the most senior-reading part of the
  repo. Write it while you are annoyed — that is when it is honest.

The concrete symptoms, so you recognise them:

- **Retrieval returns the right document and the wrong part of it.** Chunk boundaries cut a
  breaking-change note away from the heading that gives it meaning → this is what metadata
  enrichment is for; carry the heading into the chunk before you touch the retriever.
- **Vector search alone misses exact identifiers.** A question about a renamed export is a
  keyword question and embeddings blur it → that is the case hybrid retrieval exists for, and
  the eval table is where you prove it rather than assume it.
- **Citations drift.** The answer is right and points at the wrong line, because the citation was
  reconstructed from text instead of carried through the pipeline → keep source offsets on the
  chunk from ingestion onward. A wrong citation is worse than none.

The 2-hour rule from [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) applies to the plumbing
here, not to answer quality: quality is a measurement loop, and it is supposed to take the whole
second week.

## The interview angle

This is the project that makes the whole spine a different conversation. It lets you answer "how
would you build RAG over changing documentation?" as an ingestion, indexing, retrieval and
evaluation problem with numbers attached, not as a framework quickstart. The system-design rep
buried in it is a two-stage retrieval pipeline with a quality, latency and cost trade-off at
every stage. Resume line: *hybrid retrieval over package changelogs with fusion merging,
reranking, inline citations, and published eval scores before and after each stage.* The
follow-up a good interviewer asks is how it stays fresh as changelogs are published; the honest
answer is that re-ingestion runs on the queue from
[`P5.2`](../../phase-5/projects/P5.2.md), and that fine-tuned embeddings and agentic retrieval
loops were cut on purpose.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no multi-modal, no agentic retrieval loops, no fine-tuned embeddings, no
crawling the whole registry. The corpus cut is the load-bearing one: a wide corpus turns a
two-week build into an infrastructure project and blows the storage ceiling.

**Stretch** — contextual retrieval: generate a context sentence per chunk before embedding, and
report the eval delta. Only after the ship checklist is green, and only if week 39 did not slip.
