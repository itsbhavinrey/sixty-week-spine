# P4.3 — `driftwatch` · driftwatch layer 5

> One trace that follows a scan from the API edge, through Postgres, out to the npm registry, and back.

**Phase 4** · ships week 24 · Grafana Cloud + Axiom + Sentry · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P4.3`
(`plan/01-roadmap.md` titles this layer `driftwatch` Layer 5: OpenTelemetry.)

## Why this one

`driftwatch` now has a public API from [`P2.2`](../../phase-2/projects/P2.2.md) and, after
[`P3.4`](../../phase-3/projects/P3.4.md), multi-tenant auth on top of it. That is the first
version of the spine where a failed request is not self-explaining: a scan crosses the API
edge, a query layer enforcing org permissions, and an outbound call to a registry you do not
control. This layer adds traces, metrics and error reporting across all three hops. It comes
before load testing on purpose, and the roadmap's reordering table says why: a load test tells
you *that* the system broke, while traces are the only thing that tells you *where*. That is
what makes [`P5.4`](../../phase-5/projects/P5.4.md) (`breakpoint`) worth running at all —
without this you get a graph with a cliff and no way to explain the cliff. Week 23 ships
nothing and is the learning week; treat weeks 23 and 24 as one build.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have instrumented a service across a process boundary yourself and can explain how
  trace context travels in a request header, and what happens when one hop drops it.
- You will have chosen a sampling strategy and can defend it — what you lose, and why keeping
  everything is the wrong default even at low volume.
- You can say which of the three signals answers which question, why they live in different
  places, and what following the semantic conventions buys the next reader.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **No spans arrive and nothing errors** — exporters fail quietly, and a batching exporter in
  a short-lived request handler is torn down before it flushes → get the console exporter
  working first, then swap the destination; force a flush before the handler returns. Two-hour
  rule fires here, because "nothing happened" gives you nothing to read.
- **The trace splits in two at the outbound call** — the context header is not being injected
  into your registry request, so the remote side starts a fresh trace → instrument the HTTP
  client rather than the handler, and assert on the outgoing header in a test.
- **Attributes look right and group wrong** — hand-written names collide with the conventions
  the backends key their views on, and high-cardinality values quietly get expensive → follow
  the conventions; keep identifiers out of metric labels.
- **The hosted backends' no-card plans do not stretch as far as the plan assumed** — the real
  risk in this phase: retention windows and ingest ceilings at hosted observability vendors
  are the thing here most likely to have moved since the plan was written → read
  [`../verified/`](../verified/) (newest last) and [`../topics.md`](../topics.md) *before*
  pointing a deployed service at them, and re-run `verify-phase` if the newest audit is old.

## The interview angle

This is the "what happened at 3 a.m." question, and it separates candidates who have debugged
from a dashboard from ones who have only read about one. The system design rep is context
propagation and sampling: how a trace ID survives a network boundary, and how you keep a
useful picture without keeping every request. Resume line: *distributed tracing across a
serverless API, Postgres and an external registry, with metrics, logs and errors in their own
backends.* The follow-up is usually about running your own collector; the honest answer is
that you cannot, on serverless with no card, and the local demo is the stretch that shows you
know what one is for.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no OTel Profiles; the spec rules them out as not production-ready, and
[`../verified/`](../verified/) is where you check whether that has changed. No deployed
collector: it needs a VPS you are not buying. No vendor agent, which hides the part you are
here to learn.

**Stretch** — run a collector locally under Docker Compose with a processor that redacts
sensitive fields, recorded with asciinema. One of the serverless-only casualties where a
local demo is the honest proof; it leans on [`P4.1`](P4.1.md). After the checklist, not
instead of it.
