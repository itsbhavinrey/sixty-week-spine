# P4.4 — `slo-kit`

> SLOs as code for driftwatch, burn-rate alerts that fire, a public dashboard, and a postmortem for an outage you caused on purpose.

**Phase 4** · ships week 25 · Grafana Cloud (public dashboard) · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P4.4`

## Why this one

This is where the phase title cashes out. "Ship it like you'll be paged" stays a slogan until
something defines what being paged should mean, and that is an objective with an error budget
attached. [`P4.3`](P4.3.md) put the signals in place, which makes this cheap: error budgets
and burn-rate alerting are senior-reading vocabulary that cost a few hours once the telemetry
exists, and weeks if you try first. It sits last in the phase for a reason — weeks 26 through
30 are the month-7 wall that
[`plan/03-reality-check.md`](../../../plan/03-reality-check.md) warns about, and this is the
last project before it. Week 26 ships nothing and is a buffer: spend it publishing rather
than catching up, and the observability post is the one to write.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have written three SLIs yourself and can explain why each measures something a user
  would notice rather than something a server finds easy to report.
- You can explain multi-window multi-burn-rate alerting — why a single threshold either pages
  you constantly or too late, and what the second window is for.
- You will have committed the alerting definitions as code, and written a blameless
  postmortem for an outage you caused that you can walk without blaming a person.

**Definition of done** — the ship checklist in [`../README.md`](../README.md) is green, the
dashboard link is public, and the README carries the full postmortem: timeline, contributing
factors, and what you changed as a result.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **Your SLI stays green while the thing is broken** — a success rate computed from responses
  the service managed to return cannot see the requests that never got one, and a mean hides
  the tail → measure at the outermost edge you can; use a tail percentile, not an average.
- **Alerts either never fire or fire on nothing** — windows and thresholds interact in ways
  the formula does not make obvious, and your traffic is too thin for a short window to mean
  anything → test the expressions against recorded data before trusting them, and prefer a
  longer short window on a low-traffic service. Two-hour rule fires here; alert-expression
  debugging is the classic way this week disappears.
- **The deliberate outage is harder to stage than to cause** — a break that destroys your own
  evidence, or burns a shared quota on the observability side, teaches you nothing → decide
  the break, the blast radius and the rollback first, and read [`../verified/`](../verified/)
  and [`../topics.md`](../topics.md) before you trigger it. Hosted observability plans without
  a card are the likeliest thing in this phase to have moved since the plan was written, and a
  staged incident is the wrong time to find a ceiling.
- **The postmortem drifts into self-criticism** — blameless is a format, not a mood → name
  contributing factors and the missing guardrail, not the mistake.

## The interview angle

This answers "what is your SLO, and did you meet it", then the better version: what happens
when the budget is gone. Very few candidates at the mid-level bar have a real postmortem to
point at, which makes this the strongest artifact in the phase. The system design rep is
reliability as a design input — how an availability target changes what you build, and why
chasing another nine costs more than the failure it prevents. Resume line: *SLOs and
burn-rate alerts as code for a live service, with a public dashboard and a published
postmortem for a deliberate outage.* The follow-up asks how the error budget changes what you
ship; the honest answer is that a one-person service has no release freeze to enforce, and
saying so beats inventing one.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no on-call rotation tooling, which needs people to rotate; no
incident-management service, which is a signup rather than a skill; no delivery-metrics
dashboard, which measures your habits and proves nothing about the service. Each cut is half
a week you do not have in a six-week phase carrying four projects.

**Stretch** — a CI job that comments the error-budget burn on every pull request, which leans
on [`P4.2`](P4.2.md). Only once the postmortem is written; the postmortem is the artifact and
the bot is decoration.
