# P4.2 — `pipeline`

> A reusable GitHub Actions workflow that tests, scans, generates an SBOM, signs the artifact, and deploys - that other repos can call in three lines.

**Phase 4** · ships week 22 · GitHub Actions reusable workflow · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P4.2`

## Why this one

[`P0.1`](../../phase-0/projects/P0.1.md) shipped one CI workflow, and you have been
copy-pasting it into every repo since — a dozen slightly divergent copies, where fixing one
fixes none of the others. This project pays that debt back by turning the ad-hoc workflow
into a `workflow_call` one that other repos consume in three lines. It sits here rather than
earlier because it needs something worth scanning and signing, and the container images from
[`P4.1`](P4.1.md) are that artifact. Everything shipped after this week deploys through it,
so getting it right amortises across the remaining projects.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have written a reusable workflow with declared inputs and secrets yourself and can
  explain why a caller passes permissions rather than inheriting them by default.
- You can explain the difference between static analysis, dependency and container scanning,
  and secret scanning — three tools, three different classes of finding, one gate.
- You will have produced an SBOM and diffed two releases of it, and can say what that diff
  tells a security reviewer that a changelog does not.
- You will have signed an artifact without managing a key, and can explain what a public
  transparency log adds beyond the signature itself.

## Build plan

| 1 | Extract the existing CI into a `workflow_call` workflow with inputs | first hour | One repo calls it and the build still passes |

**Definition of done** — the ship checklist in [`../README.md`](../README.md) is green, at
least two of your existing repos have deleted their local copy and call this one, and an
outsider can verify a signature themselves by pasting one command.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **The signing step works in the workflow's own repo and fails when called from another** —
  keyless signing needs a workload identity token, and the calling workflow must grant that
  permission and pass secrets explicitly; a reusable workflow does not inherit either → set
  the permission in the *caller*, and read the reusable-workflow docs rather than a blog. Put
  the two-hour rule on this one: the failure looks like a credentials problem and is a
  permissions-declaration problem.
- **The scanners return a wall of findings and the pipeline is red forever** — default
  severity thresholds surface transitive dependency noise you cannot fix → decide a policy
  once (which severities fail, which are recorded), write it in the README, and treat the
  ignore file as a reviewed artifact rather than a way to make red go away.
- **Matrix jobs multiply and the run takes longer than the build it protects** — every axis
  you add multiplies, and scanners are the slow steps → run scanners once, not per matrix
  cell, and keep the matrix to what genuinely differs.
- **A consumer repo breaks when you change an input** — you now maintain an interface → pin
  callers to a tag and change inputs additively.

## The interview angle

This is the supply-chain conversation, which sits high on the application-security lists and
is what a platform or backend interviewer reaches for to find out whether you have shipped
somewhere with a compliance burden. The system design rep is artifact provenance: how do you
know the thing running in production is the thing your CI built, and what breaks that chain.
Resume line: *a reusable CI workflow — test, SAST, dependency and secret scanning, SBOM,
keyless signature, deploy — consumed by multiple public repos.* The follow-up is usually
about policy enforcement: blocking a deploy on an unsigned artifact. The honest answer is
that the gate is the stretch and you cut it, because a gate you cannot bypass in an emergency
is its own outage.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no self-hosted runners (their cost position is in
[`../verified/`](../verified/), and hosted runners on public repos need no card); no second CI
system, which teaches you YAML dialects rather than concepts; no findings-aggregation server,
which is a deployment of its own; no policy gates, which is the stretch. Keep every repo
public — Actions is metered differently on private ones, and public is the portfolio strategy
anyway.

**Stretch** — provenance attestation plus a verification step that fails the build on an
unsigned artifact. Only after the checklist is green.
