# P1.3 — `wire`

> Paste a URL, get the full story: DNS chain, TLS handshake, certificate chain, negotiated HTTP version, every header, and what your caching setup is actually doing.

**Phase 1** · ships week 7 · Cloudflare Workers · difficulty Medium
**Spec:** [`plan/01-roadmap.md`](../../../plan/01-roadmap.md) → search `P1.3`

## Why this one

Someone deploys a site, copies a caching header from a snippet, and cannot find out what a CDN
will do with it. This takes a URL and says so in English, next to the DNS chain, the negotiated
protocol and the certificate chain with its expiry. It exists because of the spec's reordering
argument: wire-protocol literacy gates two later phases. Every auth project in phase 3 —
[`P3.1`](../../phase-3/projects/P3.1.md) through
[`P3.4`](../../phase-3/projects/P3.4.md) — argues about what travels over the connection and who
can read it, and cookie attributes, token placement and redirect safety are header behaviour,
which is what this makes visible. It spans weeks 6 and 7: week 6 is headers and caching, week 7
is DNS, TLS and protocol negotiation.

## What you'll be able to do after

- You will have drawn a TLS handshake yourself and can walk it through — what each round trip
  buys, what a resumed connection skips, and what changes when the transport under HTTP is not
  TCP.
- You will have parsed a certificate chain in code and can say what "trusted" means: which link
  signs which, and what a browser checks.
- You will have read `Cache-Control`, `ETag` and `Vary` closely enough to predict CDN behaviour,
  and can name the combinations that quietly disable caching.

## Where you'll get stuck

- **The runtime will not give you the low-level facts.** A Workers fetch hands you a response,
  not a socket, so cipher suite, negotiated protocol and the raw chain may not be exposed the
  way you expect → establish what the platform surfaces, per [`../topics.md`](../topics.md) and
  [`../verified/`](../verified/), before designing the output. Most likely place for the 2-hour
  rule to fire: write "I expect field X on the response, I observe Y" rather than guess at
  APIs.
- **Certificate parsing eats an afternoon.** Chains arrive encoded, out of order, sometimes
  incomplete because the server omits an intermediate → treat an incomplete chain as a finding
  your tool reports, not a parser bug.
- **Your caching explanation is confidently wrong.** Directives interact, and one `Vary` or a
  cookie on the response makes everything above it irrelevant → write the rules as an ordered
  decision list you can defend, and test each against a site you already understand. Redirects
  matter too: decide whether you report the final hop or every hop.

## The interview angle

This is the debugging question: it works locally and not through the CDN, or a request is slow
and nobody knows which layer owns it. You can name the layers in order and what each
contributes. The system-design rep is caching — what is cacheable, keyed on what, invalidated
how. Resume line: *a deployed URL inspector reporting the DNS chain, TLS handshake and
certificate chain, negotiated protocol and a plain-English CDN caching verdict.* The follow-up
is the newer transport's round-trip advantage; measuring it is the stretch goal.

## Scope discipline

**Out of scope** — no security scoring (a letter grade invites arguments and teaches nothing),
no historical tracking, no accounts, no port scanning. Each adds storage or auth this project
does not have.

**Stretch** — the round-trip difference between the two HTTP versions, measured. Only after the
ship checklist is green.
